# History — World War I

## Causes of World War I

World War I (1914–1918) was triggered by a complex web of causes:

### Immediate Trigger
The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary in Sarajevo on June 28, 1914, by Gavrilo Princip, a Serbian nationalist. Austria-Hungary issued an ultimatum to Serbia, leading to a chain of alliance activations.

### Long-term Causes (MAIN)
- **Militarism** — European powers built large standing armies and navies, especially Britain and Germany.
- **Alliance system** — Triple Entente (France, Russia, Britain) vs. Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy).
- **Imperialism** — Competition for colonies created tensions, particularly in Africa and the Balkans.
- **Nationalism** — Ethnic groups sought independence; Slavic nationalism threatened Austria-Hungary.

## Major Events
- Schlieffen Plan and Western Front trench warfare
- Battle of the Somme (1916) — over one million casualties
- Entry of the United States (1917)
- Russian Revolution (1917) — Russia withdrew from the war
- Armistice signed November 11, 1918

## Consequences
The Treaty of Versailles (1919) imposed heavy reparations on Germany. The war redrew the map of Europe, dissolved empires (Ottoman, Austro-Hungarian, Russian, German), and led to the League of Nations.

## Key Terms
Alliance, assassination, trench warfare, nationalism, imperialism, armistice, reparations.
